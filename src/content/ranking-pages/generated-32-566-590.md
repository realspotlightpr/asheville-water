### Define the question before choosing equipment

Record the water source, affected taps, hot-versus-cold pattern, timing, weather or plumbing changes, household demand, and existing equipment. For this topic, document pH, alkalinity, metals, stagnation pattern, and hot-versus-cold comparison. Appearance, taste, odor, staining, scale, or a sales demonstration alone cannot establish water safety or treatment performance.

### What matters for metallic or acidic water taste

The practical concerns include corrosion, fixtures, water heaters, source chemistry, or treatment interactions. A useful plan separates source-water conditions from premise plumbing and distinguishes drinking-water exposure from whole-home nuisance or equipment effects. Health-related decisions require an appropriately certified laboratory and current guidance from the relevant utility, health department, or environmental agency.

### Compare an evidence-matched response

Potential responses include plumbing correction, neutralization, flushing, or targeted treatment after representative testing. Compare the exact job, validated or certified claim, inlet limits, service flow, capacity, pressure loss, drainage, power, wastewater, consumables, sanitation, monitoring, warranty, and local service. No treatment stage should be added without a defined purpose and a way to verify it.

### Post-Treatment Verification checklist

Verification repeats the decision-driving measurement at a representative tap under comparable operating conditions. Keep the original result, sampling location, model and media details, settings, installation or service date, and follow-up measurement together. If results conflict with symptoms or change unexpectedly, investigate sampling, plumbing, source conditions, and equipment operation before assuming one cause.
