# Comparison Drafts 71–80

## 71. Whole-Home vs. Under-Sink Filtration
**Slug:** `/whole-home-vs-under-sink-water-filter/` · **Query:** whole house vs under sink water filter

Whole-home treatment serves water throughout the house; under-sink treatment focuses on one drinking tap. Compare the actual concern, desired coverage, flow, space, cost, and maintenance. Some homes use whole-home carbon or softening for general use plus reverse osmosis for drinking water. The technologies should have distinct jobs rather than duplicate each other.

## 72. Salt-Based vs. Salt-Free Water Treatment
**Slug:** `/salt-vs-salt-free-water-treatment/` · **Query:** salt based vs salt free water softener

Ion-exchange softeners reduce calcium and magnesium and require regeneration. “Salt-free” products generally use other methods intended for scale behavior and may not produce softened water. Compare measured outcome, certifications, operating limits, flow, maintenance, wastewater, and homeowner expectations. The right answer depends on whether the goal is true softening or scale management.

## 73. City Water vs. Well Water Treatment
**Slug:** `/city-water-vs-well-water-treatment/` · **Query:** city water vs well water filtration

Municipal water arrives with utility treatment and reporting; private-well owners manage their own testing and maintenance. City-water goals may involve disinfectant taste, hardness, or drinking-water preferences. Well-water plans may address geology, sediment, iron, odor, pH, or microorganisms. Neither source has one universal treatment package.

## 74. Carbon vs. Catalytic Carbon Filters
**Slug:** `/carbon-vs-catalytic-carbon-filter/` · **Query:** catalytic carbon vs activated carbon

Activated and catalytic carbon products differ in manufacturing and intended performance. Selection depends on the target compound, water chemistry, contact time, flow, capacity, and third-party certification. “Catalytic” should not be treated as automatically better for every home. Compare specific product data and maintenance requirements against the identified goal.

## 75. UV vs. Chemical Well-Water Disinfection
**Slug:** `/uv-vs-chemical-water-disinfection/` · **Query:** UV vs chlorine well water treatment

UV treats water as it passes the lamp and typically needs clear water, correct flow, reliable power, lamp maintenance, and monitoring. Chemical disinfection may provide residual protection but requires dosing, contact time, storage, and management. Neither approach fixes a damaged well or contamination pathway. Testing and sanitary inspection should guide the decision.

## 76. Choosing a Sediment Filter Micron Rating
**Slug:** `/sediment-filter-micron-rating/` · **Query:** sediment filter micron rating

Micron rating describes particle size, but nominal and absolute ratings are not identical. Finer filtration can capture smaller material while clogging faster and reducing pressure. Identify particle size and load, then consider staged filtration, housing size, flow, and replacement access. The smallest number is not automatically the best design.

## 77. Single-Tank vs. Dual-Tank Water Softeners
**Slug:** `/single-vs-dual-tank-water-softener/` · **Query:** single tank vs dual tank water softener

Single-tank softeners suit many homes and regenerate during scheduled low-use periods. Alternating dual-tank systems can provide continuous softened-water capacity and may suit high or unpredictable demand. Compare peak flow, daily use, space, cost, regeneration efficiency, complexity, and service availability rather than assuming more tanks always mean better performance.

## 78. Bottled Water vs. Reverse Osmosis
**Slug:** `/bottled-water-vs-reverse-osmosis/` · **Query:** bottled water vs reverse osmosis

Bottled water offers portability but creates recurring purchase, storage, transport, and container waste. A home RO system provides treated water at one tap but requires installation, filter changes, membrane care, and some reject water. Compare documented water quality, product specifications, convenience, annual cost, and maintenance responsibility.

## 79. Point-of-Entry vs. Point-of-Use Treatment
**Slug:** `/point-of-entry-vs-point-of-use-water-treatment/` · **Query:** point of entry vs point of use water filter

Point-of-entry equipment treats water near the home’s main supply; point-of-use equipment treats one fixture. Choose coverage based on where exposure or aesthetic concerns occur, required flow, and treatment technology. A combined design may use whole-home pretreatment to protect plumbing and a dedicated drinking-water system for a separate objective.

## 80. Backwashing Filters vs. Cartridge Filters
**Slug:** `/backwashing-vs-cartridge-water-filter/` · **Query:** backwashing filter vs cartridge filter

Cartridge filters are compact and replaced when loaded. Backwashing filters use media and periodically reverse flow to clean or reclassify the bed, requiring drainage, controls, and suitable flow. Compare sediment load, treatment goal, pressure, space, maintenance, wastewater, and long-term consumables. Each format works best within a specific design.
